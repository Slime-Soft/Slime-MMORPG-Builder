# Tannery — massing spec

Transcription of the "TANNERY" icon, region by region.

## Transcription

**The building.** Dark and low. Its FRONT IS OPEN — a wide, deep roof carried on
timber posts with no front wall, so you look straight into the workshop. Back
and sides are closed: a stone base with dark timber boarding above. The roof is
a low, wide gable of dark shingles with exposed rafters and a heavy ridge beam.

**The chimney.** A tall STONE chimney at the right-hand end, smoking.

**The hides.** The identity feature. Large pale tan SKINS stretched on
rectangular frames, hanging under the front eaves, plus one on a standing frame
out in front. They are the brightest thing in an otherwise dark building.

**Inside.** A warm lit interior, a work table with tools.

**On the ground.** Barrels on the right, an open vat/tub, tools, a low stone
apron under it all.

## Identity features — checked one by one

1. **Open front** — deep roof on posts, no front wall.
2. **Low wide DARK gable roof** with exposed rafters and a ridge beam.
3. **A tall stone chimney** at one end.
4. **Stretched hides on frames** hanging under the eaves, and one on a
   free-standing frame in front. Pale tan against the dark timber.
5. **Vats and barrels** on the ground.
6. A dark brown/grey palette — the darkest building in the set.

## How it must differ from the carpenter

Both involve an open shelter. The carpenter is a CLOSED stone workshop with a
separate lean-to shed and a bare scaffold beside it; the tannery is a single
open-fronted shed whose own roof is the shelter, hung with hides. Different
silhouette, and much darker.

## Scale anchors

Barrel ≈ 0.9 m, vat ≈ 1.2 m across, hide frame ≈ 1.6 × 1.9 m, eaves ≈ 3.6 m,
ridge ≈ 5.9 m, chimney ≈ 7.5 m, building ≈ 9 m long.
