# Blacksmith — massing spec

**Rewritten 2026-08-02 after the second rejection.** The previous version of this
file described "a stone forge hall, front open, roof on piers" and "a hearth +
chimney attached to the hall's outer flank". **Neither of those is in the
reference.** They were invented, the model was built faithfully from them, and
it came out looking nothing like the picture — with the forge apparently buried
inside the next-door building.

The lesson: this file must be a **transcription of what is visible**, listed
region by region, not a description of what the building "is". Writing "forge
hall" instead of "a second closed wing" is the entire failure.

## Transcription of the reference, region by region

**Left wing.** A closed half-timbered house. Stone-block ground floor, cream
upper storey with brown timber framing. Two small square windows upstairs, one
down. Slate blue-grey roof with visible shingle courses. Gable faces left.

**Right wing.** A second closed house, set at RIGHT ANGLES to the first and
slightly behind it, so the two form an L. Bigger and with a noticeably STEEPER
roof than the left wing. Same materials: stone below, timber-framed cream above.
A **small square chimney sits on its ridge** — modest, roughly the size of a
window, with a pale cap. Nothing larger.

**The forge.** A FREE-STANDING masonry structure standing on the ground, in
front of and to the right of the right wing, clear of both buildings. It is:
a stone base block, a large round-arched mouth with fire glowing orange inside,
and a **tapering (conical/pyramidal) stone hood** above it narrowing into a
short stack. Roughly 1.9 m wide and ~3 m tall overall — about one and a half
times a person. **It touches no wall.**

**On the ground, all free-standing.** A dark anvil, prominent, front-centre-left
of the forge. A small three-legged stool. A workbench at the far left. Two or
three barrels. A wide tub heaped with black coal, to the right of the forge.
Several pale cut logs lying on the ground right of the forge.

## Identity features — checked one by one against the render

1. **Two CLOSED wings in an L**, ridges perpendicular. No open bay anywhere.
2. **The right wing is bigger with a steeper roof** than the left.
3. **A small chimney on the right wing's ridge.** If a chimney is a large
   masonry mass, it is wrong.
4. **The forge stands free on the ground**, separated from both wings by a
   visible gap you can see ground through.
5. **The forge has a tapering hood** narrowing to a short stack — that silhouette
   is what makes it read as a forge rather than as a fireplace.
6. **Fire visible in the arched mouth.**
7. **Anvil prominent and free-standing**, plus stool, barrels, coal tub, logs.

## Explicit non-features — invented in earlier attempts, not in the image

- No open-fronted hall, no roof on piers, no lintel bay.
- No large masonry chimney stack attached to a building.
- No lean-to awning, no wall-mounted bellows, no hanging bracket sign.

## Scale anchors

Barrel ≈ 0.9 m, anvil face ≈ 0.75 m, stool ≈ 0.45 m, doorway ≈ 2.1 m.
Forge overall ≈ 3.0 m tall. A 1.8 m figure is a little taller than the forge's
arch crown.
