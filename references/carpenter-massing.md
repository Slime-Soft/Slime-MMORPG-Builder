# Carpenter / lumber workshop — massing spec

Transcription of the top-middle building on reference sheet 3, region by region.

## Transcription

**The building.** A stone-walled workshop, single tall storey, with a slate
gable roof whose ridge runs left-to-right. A tall square chimney rises through
the roof near the middle. A plain plank door in the stone front. Small window
to the left of it. It is CLOSED — the openness in the reference is the shed,
not the building.

**The scaffold.** Standing OFF the building's left end: a tall open timber
frame — four uprights with horizontal ties and diagonal braces, rising to about
roof height, carrying nothing. It is a bare frame, open on all sides, and it is
the single most distinctive thing in the image.

**The shed.** A lean-to along the RIGHT of the building: a plank roof on timber
posts, sloping down away from the wall, open on all sides, with the workshop's
goods stacked underneath and beyond it.

**On the ground.** Under and around the shed: several barrels (4+), stacked
sawn boards laid flat in a pile, a stack of round logs, a low workbench with
tools. In front of the building: more cut logs lying loose, and pale wood
shavings.

## Identity features — checked one by one

1. **Stone walls, single tall storey**, slate gable roof, ridge left-to-right.
2. **A tall square chimney** through the roof.
3. **A bare open timber SCAFFOLD** standing clear of the building's left end.
4. **A lean-to shed** down the right side, plank roof on posts, open.
5. **Barrels, stacked boards, log piles** under and around the shed.
6. **Loose logs and shavings** on the ground in front.
7. Door clear, nothing embedded in a wall.

## Explicit non-features

- Not half-timbered — this one is STONE, unlike the tailor and blacksmith house.
- No two-storey jetty, no dormers, no shopfront.
- The scaffold carries no roof and no platform; it is an open frame.

## Scale anchors

Barrel ≈ 0.9 m, log ≈ 0.3 m thick, doorway ≈ 2.1 m, eaves ≈ 4.2 m, scaffold
≈ 5.5 m tall, building ≈ 8 m long.
